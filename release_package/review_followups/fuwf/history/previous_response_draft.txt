Thank you for identifying the three remaining gaps. We conducted additional original-source checks and a diagnostic using matched information. They support a narrower, verifiable dataset contribution; they do not establish complete causal process tracing or KG superiority.

1. Reliability and completeness of process relationships.

We fixed a bidirectional sample across five relationship types and RAN1–5: 500 planned slots yielded 491 selected cases; nine unfilled slots were recorded without replacement. We compared original CR covers, meeting reports, and liaison register cells with the frozen graph, recording matches, differences, and unresolved cases separately. These checks are distinct from conversion-lineage coverage and expert validation.

For MODIFIES_SECTION, extending source access to original WG ZIP members while retaining the same 50 graph-first CR records yielded 26 exact affected-clause-set matches, three differences, and 21 unresolved cases. The differences include release bindings and require revision/snapshot reconciliation. The separate 50 source-first CR cases yielded 10 matches, 19 differences, and 21 unresolved cases: those 19 native covers had clause fields absent from the matched frozen target sets, pending snapshot binding. We do not claim population precision or completeness. For the 50 source-first SENT_TO cases, normalization yielded 37 matches, seven identity differences (six WG aliases and one merged recipient), and six unresolved/excluded cases.

We also separated citation from decision meaning. AI readings of seven available original contexts among 19 fixed cases identified scoped endorsement/agreement, explicit non-pursuit, discussion basis, and competing proposals; twelve contexts remained unavailable. No human expert assessment is claimed. One bounded example is R1-2210700: the native meeting report explicitly records agreement to the final CR, and its native CR cover lists TS 38.213, Rel-17, clause 18, matching the graph's affected-clause target. The other five positive citations lacked recoverable native CR covers in the inspected source frames. This demonstrates one auditable association, not subsequent incorporation or a complete causal chain. Meeting/agenda coverage remains unresolved in many sampled cases.

Our increment concerns meeting and routing records beyond specification content; the inspected artifact difference does not establish practitioner benefit or comparative QA superiority. 3. Structural asymmetry of the RAG–KG comparison.

We agree that the original comparison does not isolate a representation advantage. We tested all 25 RAN1–5 observed-group/recipient-group combinations over the same 47 verified records. Complete linearized text, ten BM25-retrieved whole-record passages, and generated read-only SQL used the same facts and returned the same fields. We requested gpt-6.1-sol with medium reasoning. Each task/arm had one attempt, with no scientific retries; failures scored zero.

Mean record-set F1 was 0.920 for complete text, 0.850 for capped text, and 0.840 for SQL, with 2, 2, and 4 failed calls respectively. Ten tasks had empty answers; on the fifteen nonempty tasks, the corresponding F1 values were 0.867, 0.750, and 0.733. All successful complete-text and SQL calls were exact. Seven failures had thread-creation errors and one timed out without that diagnostic. Accordingly, these results provide no evidence of a structured-representation advantage. This small diagnostic uses linearized records, not original-document RAG or a new KG benchmark. It supports withdrawing the KG-superiority interpretation of the original gap.

We ask that the contribution be reassessed on this bounded basis: a reusable process-record dataset and executable queries, accompanied by original-source evidence and explicit unresolved cases. Expert validation, broader completeness, and practitioner utility remain open.
